Keeping Your Marlborough Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Marlborough Homeowners Face

Whatever type of water heater your Marlborough home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
