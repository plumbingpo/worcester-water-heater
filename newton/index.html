How Newton Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a surprise cleanup.
