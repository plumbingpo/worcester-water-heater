If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, causes that familiar rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across Worcester, MA, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Here is what the visit covers. We flush the tank until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We flush the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
